Mapping the Moon’s minerals and caves (software)

The programs that turn orbital data into mineral maps and cave candidates. Six of them are animated to show how they work.

01

Minerals

from spectra to maps
  • Tetracorder · USGS

    Expert system that compares each spectrum with a library of laboratory spectra and assigns the closest mineral. Used on M³ data.

  • Modified Gaussian Model · MGM

    Splits a spectrum into its individual absorption bands, to tell apart different pyroxenes or olivine, for example.

  • Radiative transfer models · Hapke

    Estimate the percentage of each mineral in a mixture. The global maps of olivine, pyroxene and plagioclase from Kaguya data were made this way.

  • Lucey algorithms

    Formulas that derive iron and titanium from ratios between colour bands. Written for Clementine and still in use.

  • ENVI · PyHAT

    Environments for hyperspectral analysis that support M³ data. ENVI is commercial; PyHAT is a Python tool from USGS.

Research code · many groups

Machine learning

Neural networks and “unmixing” methods applied to M³ cubes to classify minerals automatically. Mostly research code tied to single papers, not established products.

02

Caves and pits

from images, radar and gravity
LROC team · Arizona State University

PitScan

Algorithm that searched LRO’s high-resolution images automatically for anomalous shadows. It produced most of the catalogue of over 200 pits, published as the Lunar Pit Atlas. Source

University of Kent

PITS · Pit Topography from Shadows

Calculates the depth of a pit from the shape of its shadow. Source

University of Kent

ESSA · Entrances to Sub-Surface Areas

Deep-learning model trained to recognise cavity entrances in images of the Moon and Mars. It flagged some new candidates. Source

University of Trento

Radar simulations

3D electromagnetic models that predict the radar echo of a pit with or without a conduit, to compare with the real data. The method behind the 2024 result at Mare Tranquillitatis. Source

Purdue University

GRAIL gravity analysis

Gradiometry and correlation techniques that look for the linear mass deficits typical of buried lava tubes. Source

The animations are schematics. Shapes, angles and numbers are illustrative, not data.
